# What you will build

You have a small computer with a 64×32 grid of coloured LEDs on the front. Over
twelve lessons you write the Python that makes it do things — and by the end you
have built a game you can play.

Each lesson is a folder with one file in it. Open the file, change something,
press Ctrl+S, and about a second later it is running on the LEDs. There is no
Run button: **saving is running**.

| # | What you make | What you learn | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Your own words on the screen, in a colour you pick | Variables — names that hold something you can change | 10 min |
| 2 | A coloured block, anywhere you want it | Coordinates — the screen is a grid, 64 across and 32 down | 15 min |
| 3 | A block that walks across the screen | Loops — doing something over and over | 20 min |
| 4 | A block that follows the board as you tilt it | Reading a sensor, and turning its numbers into positions | 25 min |
| 5 | A block that stops dead at the walls, with a warning light | `if` and `else` — letting the program decide | 25 min |
| 6 | A ball you shove around a box by tilting it | Speed as a number you can change, and what a bounce really is | 30 min |
| 7 | A hundred stars falling down the screen | Lists — one name holding many things at once | 25 min |
| 8 | Fireworks that burst and fade when you nudge the board | Functions — writing your own commands | 30 min |
| 9 | A game: catch the falling dots on a paddle | Collisions — working out when two things are touching | 35 min |
| 10 | The same game, with the score up on the screen | Changing what the screen says while the game runs | 25 min |
| 11 | A wall of bricks you can knock down | Loops inside loops, to build a whole grid | 25 min |
| 12 | **Breakout** — paddle, ball, bricks, score and lives | Putting all of it together | 60–90 min |

**About five and a half hours in total.** Nothing is rushed, and lessons 1 to 3
are quick on purpose.

Every lesson ends with a short list of *Try these* — small changes to make and
questions to answer. They are the best part, and there are no marks for them.

**You cannot break the board by writing bad code.** The worst that happens is the
LEDs go dark and a message appears telling you which line to look at. Fix it,
save, and it carries on.
